Agency or in-house: who should run your restaurant’s marketing?

Short answer

Hire in-house when marketing is a full-time, daily job across several outlets and you can fund a small team. Use an agency when you need strategy, production and advertising skills working together from the first month. Many venues do best with both: an in-house coordinator who knows the business, and an agency that brings the specialist skills.

By Jack Brown, Founder of BOKKO · November 2026

Start with the job, then decide who does it

Restaurant marketing is several jobs. Before comparing costs, list what has to be done every month:

  • Deciding what the marketing is for this month: weekday lunch, a new menu, private events
  • Photography and short-form video that make the food and the room look as good as they are
  • Design, captions, scheduling and replying to guests
  • Paid campaigns on Meta, Google and TikTok, and the tracking behind them
  • Creators and KOLs: finding, briefing and coordinating them
  • A report that says what was spent and what came back

It is rare for one person to do all of these well. That is the real question behind “agency or in-house”.

When in-house is the right call

Hotel groups and multi-outlet restaurant groups with something to publish every day usually need people on site. An in-house team also makes sense when the brand needs constant guest interaction that only someone in the building can provide.

When an agency is the right call

A single venue, or a group that wants results before it builds a department, usually gets further with an agency. As a reference point, BOKKO Hospitality Growth is ฿85,000 + VAT a month and covers strategy, four professional shoots across six months, around 8–10 published pieces a month, account management, and Meta, Google and TikTok advertising management.

The model that works most often

An in-house coordinator handles daily stories, guest replies and approvals. The agency handles strategy, production and advertising. Each does what it is best placed to do.

FAQ

Common questions

Can an agency work alongside our in-house marketer?

Yes. It is often the strongest set-up: your marketer knows the venue and the guests, and the agency adds production, advertising and reporting.

How long before an agency shows results?

It depends on the objective and the starting point. In BOKKO’s published work, Pi Samui Beach Club tripled monthly revenue within two months and Opera Italian Restaurant within four.

What should stay in-house whatever we choose?

Replies to guests, on-the-day stories and anything that needs someone in the room. These are faster and more natural when handled on site.
