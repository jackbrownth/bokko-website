Marketing a new restaurant opening in Bangkok: what to do and when

Short answer

Start six to eight weeks before opening. Settle the positioning and shoot the content first, set up Google Maps and booking before any advertising, use creators and local campaigns in opening week, and then move from awareness to repeatable demand in months two and three.

By Jack Brown, Founder of BOKKO · November 2026

The launch timeline

WhenWhat to doWhy
6–8 weeks beforePositioning, concept and mood board; decide the audience and price point you are speaking toEverything later depends on a clear answer to “who is this for?”

Opening weekCreator visits; Meta campaigns around the venue; Google Search and Maps campaignsAwareness and discovery arrive together while the opening is news
Weeks 2–6Publish guest-facing content; ask for Google reviews; adjust campaigns to what is bookingEarly reviews and real guests replace launch hype
Months 2–3Shift budget from awareness to specific demand: weekdays, groups, signature dishesA launch is finished when the quiet services are filling

Three decisions that shape a launch

  • What is the one thing people should say about you? A signature dish, a view, a chef, a price point. Launch content should repeat it.
  • Where will people book? Phone, LINE, a booking platform. Whatever it is, every post and advert should lead there.
  • What does success look like at 90 days? Covers per week, weekday occupancy, private-event enquiries. Agree it before opening so the campaigns have a target.

FAQ

Common questions

When should marketing for a new restaurant start?

Six to eight weeks before opening. That allows time for positioning, a shoot, setting up Google Maps and booking, and scheduling creators.

Do we need influencers for a launch?

Creators help a new venue reach people quickly, provided they are chosen for the audience you want and given a clear brief. They work best alongside your own content and local advertising.

Should we advertise during a soft opening?

Keep it light. Use the soft opening to gather content, feedback and first reviews, then increase advertising once service is running as intended.
