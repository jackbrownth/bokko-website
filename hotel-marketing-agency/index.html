How we market hotels and resorts

Show the experience, not a list of facilities

For different hotels, the story might be architecture, the team, dining, wellness, the neighbourhood, exclusivity or family appeal. Content needs to show what makes the property distinctive instead of repeating a standard luxury-hotel checklist.

Our production planning considers the hotel’s brand guidelines, priority room types, facilities, guest segments and seasonal commercial calendar.

Social media and performance advertising in one plan

Hotels benefit from a joined-up system of organic content, high-quality visual assets and paid campaigns. Depending on the objective, this may include Instagram and Facebook, Meta advertising, Google Ads and dedicated campaign landing pages.

We distinguish upper-funnel interest from booking-stage intent and do not present every impression as a booking opportunity. Campaigns lead to a clear next action wherever possible.

Hotel-specific commercial objectives

Priorities can include direct booking interest, weekend occupancy, domestic travel campaigns, restaurants and bars, wedding or events enquiries, spa demand and new-property launches.

Each needs different messaging, creative and tracking. A campaign for a fine-dining restaurant inside a hotel should not be measured the same way as an overnight-stay offer.

Working with the hotel’s commercial team

Marketing works best when the agency understands occupancy patterns, lead times, packages, audience insight and operational constraints. We plan around the property’s rate strategy and sales calendar instead of running disconnected promotions.

For pre-opening properties and repositioning projects, our hospitality advisory service covers feasibility, pre-opening planning and marketing strategy.

Hotel restaurants and bars as destinations in their own right

An outlet that relies on in-house guests has a ceiling. To bring in local diners and visitors staying elsewhere, the restaurant needs its own name, content and campaigns, separate from the hotel’s feed. That is the work we did for CHON at The Siam, for Chez Sonia and Piman Terrace at Rancho Charnvee, and for Anantasila’s beachfront restaurant.

FAQ

Hotel marketing questions

Can BOKKO market luxury hotels in Thailand?

Yes. Our portfolio includes luxury hotel work in Bangkok, and creative and paid-media services are scoped to each brand’s standards.

Can BOKKO help a hotel in Thailand increase direct bookings?

Yes. Direct bookings are a common objective in BOKKO’s hotel programmes. Content, Meta and Google campaigns are built to send guests to the hotel’s own website, booking engine or enquiry channels, and are measured on direct booking interest, enquiries and revenue. For Mandarin Oriental Bangkok, one conversion-focused campaign generated more than ฿6 million in sales within 72 hours. BOKKO’s work complements a hotel’s revenue-management and OTA teams.

Can campaigns promote restaurants and bars within a hotel?

Yes. Dining and other property experiences can have their own audience, content and performance objectives.

Do you replace the hotel revenue-management team?

No. We work alongside the property’s commercial team and use agreed offers, inventory and booking pathways.

Can you help with a new hotel launch?

Yes. Launch campaigns combine positioning, content production, social distribution and media planning around a realistic opening timeline.

Which hotels and resorts has BOKKO worked with in Thailand?

Mandarin Oriental Bangkok, Renaissance Pattaya Resort & Spa, Amara Hotel Bangkok, Anantasila in Hua Hin and Rancho Charnvee Resort & Country Club in Khao Yai, as well as CHON, the Thai restaurant at The Siam.

Do you work with resorts outside Bangkok?

Yes. We have worked with resorts in Pattaya, Hua Hin and Khao Yai, and with venues in Koh Samui and Krabi.

Who manages hotel advertising campaigns at BOKKO?

The founder, Jack Brown, is personally involved in the strategy, management and optimisation of advertising campaigns across Meta, Google and TikTok, supported by an in-house team.
