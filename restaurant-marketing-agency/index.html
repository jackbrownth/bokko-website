How we market a restaurant

The guest journey starts before the reservation

Someone sees a reel, checks Instagram, searches the restaurant name, looks at Google Maps, opens the menu and finally makes contact. Weakness at any point can lose a prospective guest.

Our planning looks at the complete journey, including how an offer is explained, whether menus and booking links are easy to find, and whether paid traffic reaches a useful destination.

Food, people and atmosphere, not generic posting

We build content pillars around signature dishes, chefs, ambience, service, cocktails, special occasions and the specific audience the venue wants to attract. A premium Italian restaurant should not sound or look like a casual noodle shop.

Concept boards, photography, reels and publishing reinforce the restaurant’s positioning while making the experience understandable to new guests.

Different campaigns for different demand problems

A new opening needs discovery. An established restaurant with empty weekday tables needs demand at those times. An upscale restaurant may need to explain its private-dining offer or a chef collaboration.

The media mix can include Meta campaigns, Google Ads and local discovery improvements. Budgets and success measures follow the actual commercial challenge; blanket boosting is rarely a complete strategy.

Measurement that matters to operators

Useful measures include booking actions, calls, website menu views, direction requests, qualified enquiries, guest counts and revenue trends. Not every signal proves that an advertisement caused a sale, and our reporting makes that distinction.

If you are opening a new venue or rethinking the concept itself, our hospitality advisory work covers feasibility, pre-opening and operations.
